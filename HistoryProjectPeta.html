The History of Cloud Computing

From massive 1960s mainframes to the modern global cloud.

Timeline & Evolution

1. The Big Idea (1960s) - Mainframes & Time-Sharing

Before the cloud, computers were giant mainframes that filled entire rooms and cost millions of dollars. The problem was that only one person could use a mainframe at a time, leaving computers idle while others waited.

The breakthrough was made by scientists who came up with Time-Sharing. Multiple people could connect to one giant mainframe using basic terminals (screens plus keyboards) and share its processing power simultaneously.

Key Figure: J.C.R. Licklider envisioned an "Intergalactic Computer Network," the early concept of people and data connected globally through networks.

2. The Foundation (1970s–1980s) - Virtualization

As personal computers (PCs) became popular in the 80s, companies started buying thousands of individual servers. But another problem popped up: most servers were running at only 10%–15% capacity because each server was dedicated to just one specific task.

Virtualization Introduced: Technology (pioneered heavily by companies like VMware in the late 90s) allowed one physical computer to split its resources and run multiple virtual machines (VMs) at the same time.

3. The Shift to Services (1990s–Early 2000s) - SaaS & AWS

In the late 90s, the internet got fast enough to deliver software over web browsers instead of installing it via CDs.

  • 1999 (Salesforce): Salesforce launched the first major SaaS (Software as a Service) platform, delivering enterprise CRM software directly through a web browser.
  • 2002–2006 (Amazon Web Services / AWS): Amazon had tons of computing infrastructure built for its online store that stayed unused during non-holiday seasons. They realized they could rent out that extra computing power and storage to other businesses over the internet.
  • 2006: Amazon officially launched Amazon S3 (storage) and EC2 (virtual servers). This was the birth of modern IaaS (Infrastructure as a Service) and the official start of the modern cloud era.

4. The Cloud Goes Mainstream (2008–2010s) - Tech Giants Join In

Once Amazon proved the cloud model worked, other major tech companies jumped in:

  • 2008 (Google App Engine): Google launched GAE, pioneering PaaS (Platform as a Service), allowing developers to upload code without worrying about server setup.
  • 2010 (Microsoft Azure & OpenStack): Microsoft launched Azure to compete directly with AWS.
  • Everyday Consumers: Everyday users started using cloud platforms like Google Drive, Dropbox, iCloud, and OneDrive to save photos and documents online instead of relying on flash drives or external hard drives.
